# P01 — Complete Australian TV Buying Guide
**Editorial draft v1 — not published.** Australian model recommendations, dated prices, images and affiliate links are deliberately withheld pending product verification.

## Before comparing televisions, measure the room
A television can look impressive on a showroom wall and still be the wrong choice at home. Start with the distance from your eyes to the screen, the width of the wall or furniture, daylight and reflections, what you watch most often, and the people who will use it. The best fit is not necessarily the biggest or most expensive screen.

Record:
- Eye-to-screen distance from the usual seating position, including side seats.
- Usable wall or cabinet width and actual TV dimensions, not just the advertised diagonal.
- Windows opposite or beside the screen and viewing times.
- Free-to-air, streaming, movies, sport, gaming and music priorities.
- Soundbar clearance, HDMI devices, network access, power and antenna positions.
- Wall type and whether installation requires a professional.

## TV size and viewing distance
The advertised screen size is the diagonal, not the width. For a 16:9 display, approximate screen width is 0.872 times the diagonal and height is 0.490 times the diagonal, in matching units. A 65-inch screen is approximately 144 cm wide and 81 cm high *before* allowing for the frame, stand and cables. Always verify the actual manufacturer dimensions.

Viewing-angle guidance can help: around 30 degrees is a common starting point for everyday television, while around 40 degrees is more immersive. These are preferences, not rules. For a 16:9 TV, approximate diagonal in inches at a given distance in metres is **distance × 100 × 2 × tan(half viewing angle) × sqrt(16²+9²) / (16 × 2.54)**. Round to available sizes, then check comfort and room fit. Avoid suggesting a universal maximum size based only on distance.

## Understanding display technologies
**LED LCD:** An LCD panel uses an LED backlight. Edge-lit, direct-lit and full-array describe different lighting arrangements; performance depends on dimming implementation and processing.

**QLED:** Quantum-dot technology can improve colour performance in LCD televisions. QLED is not an alternative backlight category; a QLED TV can also be Mini LED.

**Mini LED:** Uses smaller backlight LEDs to permit potentially more precise local dimming. The number of zones alone does not determine blooming, black levels or HDR quality.

**OLED:** Self-emissive pixels enable precise black levels and wide viewing angles. Consider brightness in the actual room, static-image use, burn-in mitigation and manufacturer terms. Do not claim all OLEDs are unsuitable for bright rooms.

**RGB LED / RGB Mini LED:** Newer multi-colour backlight approaches require model-specific verification. Do not conflate them with RGB OLED or assume marketing names imply identical construction.

## Bright rooms, reflections and anti-glare
A television opposite a bright window can be difficult to watch even if it performs well in a dark test room. Look for measured reflection handling, sustained SDR brightness and appropriate screen finish. Matte finishes may diffuse reflections while affecting perceived contrast; glossy coatings can preserve clarity but show distinct reflections. Room layout, curtains and viewing angle may solve more than changing panel type.

## Resolution, motion and HDR
Most mainstream TV purchases should be assessed around 4K content and source quality, not 8K marketing. A 4K panel cannot restore detail absent from a compressed or low-resolution broadcast. Upscaling and motion processing matter for Australian free-to-air and sport.

Refresh rate is not the same as frame rate: a 120Hz display can refresh 120 times per second without the programme or game producing 120 unique frames. Check the native panel refresh rate and the actual input formats supported. HDR depends on source mastering, tone mapping, local contrast and brightness, not a logo alone. HDR10, HDR10+ and Dolby Vision support vary by model and platform.

## Sound deserves a separate decision
Thin built-in TV speakers often have limited enclosure volume, bass extension and dialogue intelligibility. Before buying a soundbar, try appropriate dialogue, night and dynamic-range settings. Stereo can create a phantom centre; a well-designed three-channel system may provide more stable centre dialogue, but channel count alone does not guarantee clarity. Dolby Atmos is not automatically a dialogue enhancer.

Match sound equipment to the room, seating and listening needs. Check ARC/eARC, audio format passthrough, lip sync and whether a soundbar blocks the screen or remote receiver.

## Gaming and connectivity
For 4K at 120Hz, check the *source, TV port, TV settings and cable* together. Look for documented HDMI capabilities, VRR, ALLM and input lag where relevant. A certified Ultra High Speed HDMI cable is a sensible starting point for common 4K/120Hz systems. Avoid equating HDMI specification numbers with guaranteed support for every optional feature. HDCP is content protection, not a cable bandwidth rating.

Wi-Fi can work well for streaming, but Ethernet may be more reliable in congested or distant rooms. Check app support, software updates, privacy controls and remote usability rather than assuming all smart TV systems are interchangeable.

## Installation, protection and care
- Unbox carefully, following the exact model's instructions; avoid pressure on the panel or twisting the chassis.
- Consider filming the unboxing, without distracting anyone handling the TV.
- Inspect and test the TV before wall mounting or long-term storage. Keep purchase records and packaging until installation is complete.
- A manufacturer's voluntary warranty usually begins under its stated purchase/delivery terms, not when the box is opened. Australian Consumer Law rights are separate.
- Fixed brackets can be simple and economical if correctly engineered and rated. Tilting and especially full-motion mounts require greater attention to leverage, joints, fixings and wall construction. Recommend professional installation for heavy, large or complex jobs.
- Follow the manufacturer's cleaning instructions. Do not use ordinary glass, alcohol or alkaline cleaners unless expressly approved for the screen. Never spray liquid directly onto the panel.
- Surge protection should account for the complete interconnected system, including applicable copper data and antenna paths. A joule number or premium price alone does not establish protection quality. Fixed electrical work requires appropriately licensed professionals.

## A practical decision route
1. Measure seating, wall width, furniture and viewing angles.
2. Identify bright-room, off-axis and content priorities.
3. Choose an appropriate size range and display technology shortlist.
4. Compare independently measured reflections, brightness, motion and local dimming for the **exact Australian equivalent**.
5. Verify audio, HDMI, networking and installation compatibility.
6. Compare current Australian models and stock; do not infer availability from overseas reviews.
7. Check ownership considerations, energy label, service and warranty terms.
8. Buy only after confirming the exact SKU, retailer terms and installation plan.

## Common questions
**Do I need OLED?** No. It can be excellent for dark-room movies and mixed viewing, but a well-engineered LCD/Mini LED TV may be preferable for certain bright rooms, sizes and budgets. Compare actual models.

**Is QLED better than Mini LED?** They describe different aspects of a display and can coexist. Judge picture performance rather than treating the labels as opposing technologies.

**Why are there black bars in movies?** A film may use a wider aspect ratio than a 16:9 TV. The bars preserve the original composition; zooming can crop the picture.

**Is a 120Hz TV worth it?** It can matter for supported gaming and motion handling. Check native refresh rate, input support and actual content.

**Should I pay more for an HDMI cable?** Pay for appropriate certified bandwidth, length and construction, not unsupported promises of sharper digital pictures.

**Should I mount the TV immediately?** No. Inspect and test it first, then use the correct rated bracket and installation method.
